# No monetization; optional anonymous support

mdwn will never charge for access: there is no billing, subscription, advertising, paywall, entitlement, or paid feature tier. The only funding surface is an optional, anonymous donation page showing static cryptocurrency addresses. This deliberately rejects both the subscription model and the earlier "first 100 pay once, then yearly" idea: a paid tier would either gate the privacy guarantees or contradict them, and taking money turns a personal-first project into a business with uptime, support, refund, and tax obligations the project's constraints rule out.

## Considered options

- **Subscriptions with a founding lifetime tier:** recurring revenue, but requires a merchant of record, entitlement state, dunning, refunds, and VAT handling, and introduces payment identity into a product built on email-free, pseudonymous Accounts.
- **Merchant-of-record checkout (for example Paddle or Lemon Squeezy):** removes tax handling for roughly 5% + a fixed fee per sale, but still makes the product a paid business and links buyers to the operator's seller identity.
- **Optional anonymous cryptocurrency donations:** covers incidental costs (domain, eventual paid infrastructure) without creating entitlements, billing state, payment identity, or support obligations, and keeps the product's only funding story consistent with its privacy values.
- **No support surface at all:** also viable, since the MVP runs on Workers Free and D1 Free; the donation page is only a convenience for people who explicitly want to contribute.

## Consequences

- A support page may exist, linked from the site footer, displaying static cryptocurrency addresses (for example BTC, a Lightning address, and XMR) with QR codes generated by local code at build time or from vendored code.
- The support page embeds no third-party script, widget, tracker, or analytics, and needs no Account, session, or personal data — consistent with the no-tracking and no-third-party-script invariants.
- Donations grant nothing: no badge, priority, feature, storage, or service guarantee. Nothing about the product changes whether or not donations arrive, and capacity exhaustion still fails closed rather than triggering paid spend.
- No donation data is stored in D1 or any operational store.
- Donor anonymity is pseudonymity relative to the product: public-chain addresses are visible and traceable, and a donor's privacy depends on their own wallet hygiene. Monero is the option that actually hides sender and amount; a Lightning address is the low-fee option for small amounts.
- Receiving cryptocurrency may carry tax or reporting obligations in the operator's jurisdiction; that is an operator concern, not a product design.
- If a mobile client is ever built, store policies (notably Apple's) may disallow in-app cryptocurrency donation links; the support surface stays on the web.
- The evaluated private donation options and their trade-offs are recorded in [`docs/support-options.md`](../support-options.md).


## Superseded in part (2026-10-12)

Two capacity and funding premises are superseded: that having no support surface is viable "since the MVP runs on Workers Free and D1 Free", and the consequence that "capacity exhaustion still fails closed rather than triggering paid spend". The maintainer has decided to pay for Workers Paid (about $5 per month targeted, $10 as an intervention boundary; the subscription itself is not yet confirmed). Usage beyond the included allowances is metered, so some capacity exhaustion becomes overage, bounded only by application limits and the operator's [intervention runbook](../operations.md#intervention-runbook), never by a provider spending cap.

Unchanged: mdwn is never monetized and has no billing, entitlement or paid tier; donations grant nothing and are never stored. The hosting subscription is the operator's cost, not a product feature.
